Why

Accounts don't die from the market. They die from the trader.

The leverage pegged at the top. The size that uses the whole account. The second entry into a loser because the first "just needs room." The stop moved at two in the afternoon because today feels different. Just this once. Every one of them is a decision made with the number in front of you.

handld takes the number away.

How capital is protected

Risk first. Then the rules hold.

You set risk and objectives with the desk once. After that the ruleset protects the capital. In the heat of a session it can cut exposure. It will not let you expand risk because the last trade stung.

  1. Objectives are set together.

    Day loss, week loss, and how much risk you are willing to put on a trade are chosen up front. That envelope is the commitment.

  2. The ruleset enforces what you already agreed.

    Size, stops, and lockouts follow the rules - not the mood after a red or a green. There is no override verb in the grammar.

  3. Exposure only shrinks in the moment.

    The desk can always reduce risk on the spot. Raising risk is slow and deliberate, never a tilt move.

  4. Stops protect capital, not ego.

    Invalidation sets the idea. The desk may place the stop further from a sweep. It never pulls it closer because it "feels safe now."

  5. Correlated risk is one book.

    Two tickers on the same idea are one exposure. A second call into a live group waits. Capital is not doubled by renaming the chart.

Who it's for

Built for funded seats on Hyperliquid - and your own book.

On-chain prop firms like Propr are not classic FX props. There is often no consistency rule, no minimum trading days, and no end-of-day flatten ritual. What ends the seat is equity: a daily loss line and a max drawdown (static or trailing), checked with floating PnL included. Touch the line and the account is gone.

handld is built for that world. You map the firm's daily and drawdown limits into the desk. We enforce a buffer inside those lines so a revenge trade or a maxed slider does not spend the seat. Same idea on your own Hyperliquid wallet: risk and objectives first, then the rules hold.

On Hyperliquid the desk signs with a trade-only agent wallet. It can place and manage orders on your behalf. It cannot withdraw funds. Your capital stays in an account you control.

Your own cash works too. The rules are the same; the profile is different. More exchanges are coming.

Not for you if you need to scale in, pick size every tick, max the leverage slider for the adrenaline, or watch live PnL to feel in control. That is a terminal. This is a leash.
